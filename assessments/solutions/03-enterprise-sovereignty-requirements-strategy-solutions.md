## Q1. Sovereignty as an investment decision (4 points)

**Worked answer:** Do not approve on-site accelerators without a measurable problem. Candidate A, **do nothing**, is a valid baseline; B, **improve existing search**, may deliver the retrieval outcome without model inference; C, **managed AI**, could be eligible for public/synthetic data when named-location, retention, supplier-administrator, logging and contract evidence are accepted; D, **on-site AI**, may be eligible but creates capacity, update, power, staffing and exit responsibilities. The presented synthetic workload does not itself justify a premises-only rule. Measure time to find a correct policy, completion/error rate, staff adoption and fully loaded operating cost compared with the baseline. Unknown evidence: managed provider backup/support and incident routes, contract rights and a reproducible exit or service-continuity test.

**Rubric:** 1 point for rejecting infrastructure-first logic, 1 for at least three meaningful alternatives including non-AI, 1 for missing evidence and authorized owner, 1 for measurable outcomes without invented ROI. Treat an unconditional vendor “PASS” as insufficient.

## Q2. Classification, authority and exception (4 points)

**Worked answer:** Record **sensitivity** (harm from disclosure/misuse of restricted documents and derivatives), **criticality** (process ability to tolerate downtime and required recovery), and **impact** (scope/severity of an adverse model or operational decision) separately for all three fictional workloads. Public FAQs can still be operationally critical, while a highly sensitive archive can have low immediate uptime requirements. Engineering drawings, retrieved chunks and embeddings inherit the binding plant-handling boundary in the hypothetical contract. The group CIO's broad country-level provider permission cannot override a narrower authorized *FACTORY-A only* commitment. A formal amendment by the contract/site authority, after legal/privacy review as applicable, must identify the data/workload scope, allowed destinations and effective date, followed by reclassification, architecture change and fresh tests.

**Rubric:** 1 point for three independent axes, 1 for derivative sensitivity and scope, 1 for correct hierarchy of applicable authority, 1 for a real exception/change-control record and retest. Do not accept “domestic cloud equals premises.”

## Q3. Three separate claims and distinct acceptance (4 points)

**Worked answer:** **Security claim (CISO):** only authorized users and supplier accounts access sensitive retrieval; run token-revocation, MFA, least-privilege, privileged-session and incident tests. **Contract/applicability claim (counsel and contract owner):** identify exact signed clauses, processor roles, supplier support terms, potential applicable law and any authorized exceptions; record specialist disposition rather than a PM guessing a statute. **Additional sovereignty claim (plant data owner/CTO):** restricted documents and all derived material stay within a named factory across inference, monitoring, diagnostics, support, updates and backups; run synthetic packet/DNS/proxy and administrator-path tests in normal and degraded modes. MFA can pass while the site routing rule fails; the diagnostic export is not authorized by a security test.

**Rubric:** 1 point for each independently testable claim (3), 1 for explicitly rejecting MFA as an offsite-transfer waiver. Award credit for noting that contractual review cannot itself prove network paths.

## Q4. Historical evidence is not a permanent platform property (4 points)

**Worked answer:** Trace **Risk: prohibited offsite derivative → REQ-SITE-v1 (issued by plant owner) → CTRL-GATE/LOG (versioned local inference and diagnostics) → TEST-NORMAL/TIMEOUT/UPDATE/SUPPORT/RESTORE → timestamped destination and privileged-session observations → CISO and plant owner disposition → BLOCKED/UNVERIFIED/PASS FOR SCOPE**. The model/gateway/telemetry version change invalidates the applicable earlier location, role and fallback evidence. Retest synthetic normal, timeout, crash, support, patch, backups and recovery with exact route configuration, DNS, proxy/firewall and logs, operator identities and hash/version records. Where operation is not observed, use **UNVERIFIED**, not green. A confirmed prohibited route is **FAILED/BLOCKED**, not merely unknown.

**Rubric:** 1 point for end-to-end IDs/owner chain, 1 for stale evidence and change trigger, 1 for normal/degraded and support/backup evidence, 1 for correct UNVERIFIED versus FAILED distinction.

## Q5. Real product evidence versus air-gap eligibility (6 points)

**Primary document checked:** Ollama maintainers, [*Ollama README*, `README.md`, main branch](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/blob/main/README.md), inspected **2026-11-21**; source publication/update date not established. Exact short excerpt: “Ollama has a REST API for running and managing models.” The README shows a loopback `localhost:11434/api/chat` request. This establishes a **documented local API example only**. Its local/cloud positioning is not a certification of physical isolation, and the README does **not** establish offline model-licence rights, disconnected installation, disabled cloud routing, safe fallback or recovery. The following Gulf Works Group workload and candidates are **fictional**; no real Ollama customer deployment is asserted.

**Worked answer:** **A** (public API) is **REJECTED** because physical disconnection forbids all network paths, regardless of national location. **B** (hypothetical on-site model requiring online licence activation) is **REJECTED AS DESIGNED**: licensing cannot require external activation during operation. **C** (Ollama-based local serving with a separately chosen model) is **UNVERIFIED / NOT READY**, not approved: a local REST example makes it a plausible *evaluation candidate*, not proof of an air-gapped implementation. Before release, inspect physical NICs/radios and out-of-band management, inventory/disable all cloud and fallback paths, confirm model-specific offline licence and provenance, verify offline installation and clean reboot, test signed media-intake custody and malware scanning, exercise offline backup/restore and rollback against RTO/RPO, evaluate task safety/quality, and independently inspect DNS, proxy, firewall and privileged-session evidence. The exact model and build versions must be recorded. A proposed control is not a passed test. If no fully disconnected, licensed, safe and recoverable option passes, record **NOT FEASIBLE UNDER CURRENT CONSTRAINTS** and use a permitted manual/non-AI alternative; changing the air-gap rule requires the actual authorized requirement owner to revise it, not an engineering tunnel workaround.

**Rubric (6 points):** 1 for A rejection; 1 for B rejection; 1 for identifying C as **unverified**, not a certified air-gap solution; 1 for at least four distinct falsifiable tests including licence/cloud-routing evidence; 1 for infeasibility and non-AI alternative when gates fail; 1 for distinguishing the vendor's documented local REST API from unproven offline/air-gap behavior and rejecting private/support tunnels. **Do not award the last point** to an answer claiming Ollama is officially air-gap certified without evidence.

## Q6. Full fictional enterprise decision and RCA (18 points)

**Recommendation:** **BLOCKED for restricted DESIGN-Q6/FACTORY-A rollout at the tested T2 gateway version.** The synthetic evidence shows an attempted prohibited offsite route, even though the firewall successfully blocked delivery. The T0 acceptance record is stale after the T1 configuration change. No actual production data exposure is established by the fictional test; security would investigate historical logs separately. An enterprise-owned GPU, good answer quality and a vendor's marketing label do not waive the signed physical-site rule.

**Six-part RCA example:** (i) *Symptom:* synthetic offsite request during model timeout. (ii) *Immediate cause:* supplier gateway points to remote API and diagnostics. (iii) *Systemic cause:* reviews approved on-prem GPUs instead of testing all data/control-plane paths after version change. (iv) *Containment:* block restricted launch; disable routes and preserve traces; assess historic risks without asserting a breach. (v) *Correction and retest:* allow local fallback or explicit safe error only; inject negative conditions and check zero forbidden attempts with signed reviewers. (vi) *Closure:* correct build, dated evidence, CISO and plant owner sign-off, contract terms verified, owner accountable for revalidation triggers. If local models, licences, recovery or quality cannot meet minimum needs, the appropriate disposition is **NOT FEASIBLE UNDER CURRENT CONSTRAINTS**, not hosted substitution.

## Module 03 lab: expected synthetic results

Run the learner workbook's `python3 labs/module-03/evaluate.py labs/module-03/cases.json` and `python3 -m unittest discover -s labs/module-03 -p 'test_*.py' -v`.
