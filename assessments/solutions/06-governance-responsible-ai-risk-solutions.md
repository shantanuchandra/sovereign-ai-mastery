## Q1. Decision rights (5 points)

Assign an implementer to deliver controls, a separate validator to challenge evidence and one accountable authority for a bounded release decision (3). Operators need tested stop/escalation authority when unsafe advice appears (1), with a manual fallback and incident contact (1). A committee's next meeting is not a containment mechanism. Award equivalent credible role structures; do not require fictional names.

## Q2. Standards, governance and observations (5 points)

Distinguish contextual framework functions, organizational AI management processes and concrete test observations for one build/use (3). Request certificate authenticity, scope, validity and exclusions where a supplier claims certification (1). Request separate workload/site evidence (1). The chapter inspected only the public ISO descriptions, not full clauses or an organizational audit; deduct for inventing certification or claiming the lab proves conformity.

## Q3. Hard constraints before preferences (5 points)

An off-site protected diagnostic copy conflicts with the unchanged premises rule (2). Block that design, repair the route or obtain a separate authorized policy change before reassessment (2). Compare costs/quality among feasible candidates only; weighted scores cannot erase a veto (1). Country location alone is insufficient.

## Q4. Explanation and intervention (5 points)

Use known incorrect/relevant manual passages to test whether an explanation faithfully supports the answer rather than simply sounding plausible (2). Test realistic operators detecting, stopping and escalating an unsafe output under time pressure; record observations and stop authority (2). A changed index reopens retrieval-linked quality/explanation evidence and other controls affected by the change; scope the retest rather than blindly inheriting v2 (1). Merely logging “human in loop” earns no intervention credit.

## Q6. Evidence transitions and root-cause analysis (15 points)

1. **Observation/status (3):** Initial v2-only evidence means UNVERIFIED. Observed v3 subgroup failure and off-site diagnostics mean BLOCKED, even alongside missing v3 oversight evidence. Calculate English 1%, Arabic 30%, overall 1.58%, disparity 29 percentage points.
2. **Direct cause (2):** The observed error distribution identifies a failing cohort, not a proven model-internal cause. Investigate retrieval relevance, source currency, Arabic parsing, query intent and unsupported generation. The diagnostic copy is a separate observed routing failure; a prompt edit cannot repair it.
3. **Systemic cause/contributing gap (2):** Aggregate-only release criteria, reused v2 evidence and an incomplete route review allowed the proposal to reach approval without suitable validation. Distinguish these from hypotheses about the error mechanism.
4. **Containment/owner (2):** The accountable release authority keeps v3 stopped; the incident commander contains exposure using the approved manual process. The platform owner disables the offending export; evaluation and Arabic domain owners investigate quality.
5. **Mitigation/retest (3):** Preserve v3 evidence. Assign fixes to distinct owners. For a proposed v4, schedule a dated retest, for example 2026-11-27, of representative language/task samples, cited support, oversight drills, all protected routes, recovery and any changed components. Link observations to actual test artifacts and version IDs. A plan or future date is not completed evidence.
6. **Residual risk/closure (3):** Missing evidence after repair remains UNVERIFIED; repeated adverse results remain BLOCKED. Only favorable complete synthetic declarations reach REVIEW_REQUIRED. Human review must address sampling, untested tasks, authenticity and production differences; no automatic compliance or safety claim. Close the incident only under the organization's defined incident criteria with scope, owner and monitoring recorded, not because the aggregate is below 2%.
