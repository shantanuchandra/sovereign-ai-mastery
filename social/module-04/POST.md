# Eight questions before expanding sovereign AI across the GCC

A national AI strategy can explain where a country wants to invest. It cannot, on its own, tell an enterprise where a specific model may run or who may access its data.

Consider a fictional industrial company evaluating an Arabic-language assistant for a UAE factory and regional expansion.

Its board should ask whether the same architecture can meet requirements across countries, what happens to voice transcription and backups, and whether local inference is enough when a vendor's support or licence path still reaches the internet.

In the exercise, an application tries to send a speech request to an external endpoint. The gateway denies the request. That is a successful network control, but the attempted fallback is still a failed application policy. The release stays on HOLD.

A physically air-gapped terminal presents an even harder boundary. A remote support tunnel or cloud licence check is not an acceptable exception.

The carousel covers eight practical decisions: national policy versus workload assurance, jurisdiction, named-premises restrictions, physical air gaps, Arabic evaluation, fallback failures, infrastructure dependencies and executive release evidence.
